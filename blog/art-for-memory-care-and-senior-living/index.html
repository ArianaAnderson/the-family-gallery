Art for memory care and senior living: a calm screen idea

Consider a quiet display of classic paintings as décor and an optional conversation prompt, with individual preferences and staff guidance first.

Published October 6, 2026 · The Family Gallery

A shared television does not always need to show news, advertising or a busy video. A slowly changing collection of paintings can be another décor choice for a lounge or activity space. In senior living and memory-care settings, the starting point should be the people in the room, their preferences and the staff responsible for their care.

Offer an option, not an obligation

Some residents may enjoy gardens, rural landscapes or familiar portraits. Others may prefer photographs, a favorite program or a screen that is off. Ask what people enjoy where possible, observe their response and make it easy to change or stop the display. Do not assume a single visual style suits everyone.

Keep the display uncomplicated

Use a comfortable brightness, avoid distracting transition effects if the player allows it, and choose an interval that gives people time to look. Consider visibility from common seating positions and whether captions are readable. Sound is not required for the Family Gallery artwork files.

A few chosen images may be more appropriate than playing a large collection at random. Your television’s player determines which folders can be selected and whether looping or manual selection is available. Staff should know how to return to ordinary viewing or turn the screen off.

Let conversation follow interest

A painting might invite someone to notice flowers, colors, weather or a familiar kind of place. Questions can stay open and optional: “What catches your eye?” or “Would you like a different picture?” Avoid treating the artwork as a memory test or insisting on a correct answer.

Check practical details with the facility

  • Get staff or facility approval for any change to shared screens or equipment.
  • Confirm USB JPEG photo playback, slideshow controls and the ability to stop playback easily.
  • Keep the drive and cables secure and out of walking paths.
  • Follow device-care, cleaning, accessibility and safety procedures.
  • Discuss licensing and multi-room use before copying the collection or deploying it across several devices.
