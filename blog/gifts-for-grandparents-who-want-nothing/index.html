Gifts for grandparents who say they don’t want anything

Choose something that fits their interests, adds little clutter and comes with time spent together rather than another task.

Published October 6, 2026 · The Family Gallery

“We don’t need anything” can be useful guidance. Instead of looking for a bigger object, consider a gift that fits the life your grandparents already enjoy: a favorite place, a familiar routine or time with family. The best choice depends on their interests, not their age alone.

Give an afternoon as well as a present

A lunch, a walk in a favorite garden or a visit with old photographs can make the gift personal. If you give something practical, offer to set it up and enjoy it together. A present should not arrive with a complicated new task attached.

Build on what they already love

For readers, consider a book by an author they already enjoy and a note about why you chose it. For gardeners, help with a small project or choose plants suited to their space. For people who enjoy art, bring a museum visit home through a book, a print or a prepared television art collection.

An art gift that uses the screen already there

Family Gallery is a $49 USB with 1,000+ captioned classic paintings. A compatible TV plays the files as a photo slideshow, with no Family Gallery subscription or ads in the artwork. The title and artist stay with each image, so a painting can prompt a story about a place, a color or a favorite artist.

It can be a good fit for grandparents who like beautiful rooms and curious conversation. It is less suitable if they do not enjoy using the television’s menus or do not have a compatible player. A small gift is only simple when it works for the recipient.

Check the television together

  1. Find the exact model and test JPEG photos from a spare USB.
  2. Confirm that slideshows and repeat are available.
  3. Offer to show how to open the artwork folder and hide playback controls.
  4. Write the few remote-control steps on a card.
  5. Ask which paintings they would like to see first.

Make it personal

A garden painting might recall a yard they tended; a landscape might resemble somewhere they traveled. Let them choose their favorites rather than assuming everyone prefers the same calm scene. If the television is not compatible, consider an explicitly USB-compatible digital photo frame, or choose a different gift.
