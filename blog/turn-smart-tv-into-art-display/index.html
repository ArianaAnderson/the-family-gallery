How to turn any smart TV into an art display

Start with a compatibility test, then find your brand’s USB photo player and choose settings that keep paintings and captions visible.

Published October 6, 2026 · The Family Gallery

A television can be useful between movies without adding another streaming service. If it supports JPEG photos from USB, it can display a local collection of paintings. “Any smart TV” is a starting question, not a compatibility promise: some models have no usable photo slideshow player.

1. Test the television before buying artwork

Find the model number and check its manual for USB JPEG playback and automatic slideshows. A USB port intended only for power or servicing will not help. Try two or three ordinary JPEG photos on a spare drive. Confirm that the player advances and repeats rather than displaying a single image indefinitely.

2. Open the right player for your brand

  • Samsung: Home → Source or Connected Devices → USB is a common route.
  • LG: use the USB prompt, or open Home Dashboard and select storage and the media player.
  • Sony: look for Media → Photo or a Media Player app, depending on the model.
  • TCL Roku TV: open Roku Media Player → Photo → USB. Google TV models use a different player.
  • Hisense: some Media interfaces use Picture; Roku, VIDAA, Google TV and Fire TV versions differ.

These are examples, not universal menu paths. Our step-by-step setup guide includes manufacturer references and more detailed brand instructions.

3. Choose pictures that fit the room

Try a landscape with a broad horizon, a garden with softer colors or a still life whose palette connects with nearby furniture. Leave the complete painting visible. A portrait can look good on a wide screen when it has a coordinated background rather than being cropped.

4. Adjust the slideshow

Choose Fit or Original, hide playback controls, and select Repeat or Loop if your TV provides it. An interval of 45–90 seconds gives a painting time to be noticed, but available options vary. Use a brightness comfortable for the room rather than assuming the brightest setting is best.

5. Make starting it easy

Keep a short note beside the remote with the steps to open the USB and start playback. Check whether the player remembers your folder and settings after switching inputs or turning off the TV. Ordinary USB playback is not a dedicated Art Mode and may need to be started again manually.
